## 1. Operating Architecture & Frictionless Setup

### The Purpose of Each Daily Portion (Why You Do What You Do)

Every single day of this 8-week curriculum is broken into a strict 3-part routine (plus Day 7 rest). Understanding **why** each portion exists prevents shortcuts and eliminates frustration:

* 🟡 **Part A: Warm-Up (10 min) — Motor Calibration & Line Conviction**
  * **The Purpose:** Cold hands instinctively draw from the wrist and fingers, producing timid, "scratchy", hairy lines. The warm-up activates the kinetic chain from your core to your shoulder. It calibrates stroke speed, arm momentum, and steady pressure so you execute clean, continuous, single-pass strokes with unhesitating confidence before touching complex spatial forms.
* 🔵 **Part B: The Drill (45–60 min) — Geometric Encoding & Spatial Discipline (The Medicine)**
  * **The Purpose:** You cannot draw from imagination what your brain cannot construct mechanically. The drill isolates a single technical variable—such as vanishing point convergence, cylinder minor axes, boolean form cuts, or fabric tension—completely removing the pressure of "making a finished artwork." It develops unconscious automaticity so your conscious mind can later focus on composition, character design, and storytelling.
* 🔴 **Part C: The Project / "Projecting" (30–45 min) — Creative Synthesis & Contextual Anchoring (The Meal)**
  * **The Purpose:** Doing drills without application produces "exercise technicians"—artists who can draw 250 perfect cubes in a vacuum but freeze when facing a blank canvas. Projecting immediately stress-tests the day's isolated drill in an authentic creative problem (e.g. turning box intersections into a spaceship or a prop). This contextual pressure burns the mechanical skill into your intuitive visual library.
* 🟢 **Day 7: The 50% Rule (Full Day) — Psychological Sustainability & Creative Play**
  * **The Purpose:** Drawn directly from Drawabox Lesson 0. If 100% of your drawing is rigorous practice and critical self-evaluation, your brain associates the sketchbook with stress and failure, leading directly to burnout. Exactly 50% of your artistic life must belong to joy, play, and curiosity—doodling, fan art, or rough ideas with strictly zero drills and zero self-critique.

### Hardware & Digital Setup Guide (Zero-Guesswork Toolkit)

#### Traditional Setup (Recommended for Maximum Growth)
* **Primary Line Pen:** `0.5mm Black Fineliner` (Sakura Pigma Micron 05, Staedtler Pigment Liner, or Uni Pin).
  * *Why:* Ink is permanent. It denies you the safety net of erasing, forces you to plan every stroke before placing it (ghosting), and visibly exposes bad habits like chicken-scratching.
* **Secondary Tone Pen:** `Standard Ballpoint Pen` (Bic Cristal Black or Blue).
  * *Why:* Ballpoint pens respond to subtle hand pressure, allowing soft volumetric under-drawings and smooth cross-hatching gradients without muddying line art.
* **Paper:** `Plain 80gsm (20lb) Unruled Copy Paper` (Letter or A4 printer paper).
  * *Why:* Bound, expensive sketchbooks trigger performance anxiety and fear of "ruining the book." Loose copy paper has zero preciousness—you can draw 10 pages without hesitation and throw away mistakes.
* **Shoulder Calibration Pad:** `A3 or 11x17 Newsprint Pad`.
  * *Why:* You cannot physically draw from your wrist across a 17-inch surface; large paper forces your entire shoulder and elbow into mechanical rotation.

#### Digital Setup (iPad / Procreate / Photoshop / Clip Studio)
* **Brush:** Hard Round brush or 6B Pencil with `Size Dynamics ON` (pressure-sensitive line width) and `Opacity Jitter OFF` (100% solid opacity).
  * *Why:* Opacity jitter causes muddy, semi-transparent overlap marks that hide structural errors. Clean form drawing requires crisp, decisive edges.
* **Stabilization / Streamline:** Set to `0%–15% (OFF/LOW)` during warm-ups and drills.
  * *Why:* Heavy smoothing algorithms fake line confidence for you. You want your real motor control to develop. Raise stabilization to 40%–60% only for final project inking.
* **Canvas Size:** `3000 x 4000 px @ 300 DPI`.
* **The Construction Golden Rule:** **NEVER zoom past 100% during construction.** Zooming in destroys your perception of overall proportion and spatial relationships, causing bobblehead figures and distorted perspective.

### The 3-Domain Scaffolding Matrix & The Goldilocks Zone (Book p. 5)

Learning only happens inside the **Goldilocks Zone** (neither too easy to cause boredom, nor too hard to cause burnout). Adjust your exercises dynamically across 3 scaffolding domains:

```
[ EASIER (High Scaffolding) ] ◄──────────────────────────────► [ HARDER (Zero Scaffolding) ]
```

| Domain | Low Challenge (Easier) | Moderate Challenge | Target Master Level (Harder) | How to Adjust When Stuck |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Information** | Ruler perspective grid | Horizon Line + Vanishing Points | Horizon Line only $\to$ **Pure Freehand Intuition** | If perspective looks wild, draw a ruler-assisted horizon line and 2 VPs. Remove them as confidence grows. |
| **2. Mechanical** | Ruler + Pencil + Eraser | Pencil Only $\to$ Ballpoint Pen | Fineliner $\to$ **Brush Pen** | If fineliner freezes you up, drop to pencil to erase and draw through. When confident, use fineliner with zero guide marks. |
| **3. Conceptual** | Static Perspective Primitives | Form Rotations & Boolean Cuts | Anatomy $\to$ Textures $\to$ **Sequential Scenes** | If rotating complex characters fails, scale back to rotating basic box mannequins before adding anatomy or costume. |

### Self-Critique & Error Debugging Rubric (The Internal Teacher)

When practicing alone, use this 4-step rubric to audit your homework at the end of every session:

1. **Perspective & Box Convergence:**
   * *The Error:* Diverging lines (lines spreading apart as they recede, creating reverse perspective).
   * *The Fix:* With a ruler and colored pen, extend the edges of your boxes backward. Verify they converge toward distant vanishing points rather than fanning outward.
2. **Ellipses & Minor Axes:**
   * *The Error:* Tilted, lopsided ellipses that do not align with cylindrical shafts.
   * *The Fix:* Always draw the minor axis line first. Ensure the ellipse is cut into two perfectly symmetrical, mirrored halves. Verify that the far ellipse is rounder (wider degree) than the near ellipse.
3. **Mannequins & Spatial Overlap:**
   * *The Error:* Flat "paper cutout" figures with stiff stick-like limbs.
   * *The Fix:* Draw wrapping cross-contour bracelets around forearms, thighs, and torsos. Overlap joints (e.g. thigh overlaps knee, knee overlaps calf) to force spatial depth.
4. **Clothing & Drapery:**
   * *The Error:* Random decorative squiggles and floating wrinkles.
   * *The Fix:* Circle the tension points first in red (pinch point at inner elbow/knee; hang point at shoulder/crotch). Every single fold must radiate directly from a tension point.

## 3. The Complete 8-Week Day-by-Day Curriculum

---

### WEEK 1: Measurements & Mechanics (Eye-Hand Coordination)

> **Primary Objective:** Train your arm to make the mark your brain intends. Measure line slope 1:1, identify positive/negative silhouettes, and master symmetrical mirroring.
> 
> **Why This Week Matters (The Purpose):** You cannot draw 3D scenes if your hand cannot reliably gauge an angle or draw a straight line. Week 1 isolates pure neuromuscular control away from 3D space: training your shoulder to replace your wrist, calibrating your eye to see 2D silhouettes without distortion, and building the confidence to commit permanent ink strokes without hesitation.

#### References & Study Material
- 📖 **brokendraw Book:** Level 1 Mechanics (pp. 6–26) — *Ex 01 Straights & Curves* (p. 7), *Ex 02 Point Coordination* (p. 8), *Ex 03 Tilting Planes* (p. 9), *Ex 04 Parallel Ellipses* (p. 10).
- 📖 **Peter Han (The Dynamic Bible):** Section 1 (pp. 4–14: *Drawing from the Shoulder, Ghosting, and Line Weight Hierarchy*).
- 🌐 **Drawabox:** [Lesson 1: Lines & Ellipses](https://drawabox.com/lesson/1) (*Ghosted Lines, Ghosted Planes, Ellipses in Planes, Funnels*).
- 📺 **Key Videos:**
  - Nathan Cooke: [Visual Communication with Nathan Cooke](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oK-4wUbBmJI)
  - brokendraw: [how to actually use boxes to draw better](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VBAJDepILYc)
  - brokendraw: [Roadmap Video — Week 1 @ 08:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zYzgxUVSpUc&t=518s)

#### Day-by-Day Schedule
- [ ] **Day 1: Shoulder Calibration & Ghosting**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Motor Activation:** brokendraw Ex 01 (p. 7) — 8x repetitions of straight lines and C/S curves from the shoulder. Wakes up the shoulder joint and locks the wrist.
  - Part B: Drill (50m) — **Purpose: Stroke Trajectory & Planning:** Drawabox [Ghosted Lines](https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/10) (2 pages) + [Ghosted Planes](https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/12) (2 pages). Teaches you to prepare the stroke trajectory in the air before committing ink.
  - Part C: Project Step (30m) — **Purpose: Workspace & Baseline Observation:** Set up your physical workspace and gather 5 household objects (e.g. coffee mug, shoe, scissors, spray bottle, tool) for Project #1.
- [ ] **Day 2: Ellipse Coordination & Coin Flips**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Minor Axis Calibration:** Peter Han *The Dynamic Bible* (p. 8) — Drawing parallel ellipses along a minor axis from the shoulder.
  - Part B: Drill (50m) — **Purpose: Ellipse Symmetry & Degree Control:** Drawabox [Tables of Ellipses](https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/13) (1 page) + [Funnels](https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/15) (1 page). Forces ellipses to stay packed tightly without pinching.
  - Part B: Drill (30m) — **Purpose: Target Landing Accuracy:** brokendraw Ex 02 (p. 8) — Point Coordination target sheet (connecting random pairs of dots without overshoot).
- [ ] **Day 3: Angle Matching & Tilting Planes**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Hatching Spacing Discipline:** brokendraw Ex 01 straight hatching lines with consistent spacing.
  - Part B: Drill (50m) — **Purpose: Tilt Angle Estimation:** brokendraw Ex 03 (p. 9) — 20 Tilting Planes in varied tilts and orientations. Trains your eye to capture acute/obtuse slopes without distortion.
  - Part B: Drill (30m) — **Purpose: Silhouette Isolation:** Start 25 Silhouettes from photo reference (trace outline shapes only; ignore all interior lines). Strips away distracting details to see pure shape.
- [ ] **Day 4: Symmetry & Positive/Negative Space**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Ellipse Fluidity:** brokendraw Ex 04 (p. 10) — Parallel Ellipses.
  - Part B: Drill (50m) — **Purpose: Shape Readability:** Complete remaining 25 Silhouettes (Total: 50 Silhouettes completed).

> **Primary Objective:** Master how light reveals surfaces. Wrap 2D textures around 3D cross-contour wireframes (volume mapping). Study master artists' mark-making to begin building your personal visual style.
> 
> **Why This Week Matters (The Purpose):** Up to this point, drawings are wireframes. Week 7 teaches how light and materials interact with form: translating the tactile feel of metal, rock, scales, and cloth into disciplined ink marks. It also introduces master artist studies so you begin cultivating your personal signature style.

#### References & Study Material
- 📖 **brokendraw Book:** *Ex 15 Texture Bar* (p. 54), *Ex 19 Volume Mapping* (p. 67).
- 📖 **Peter Han (The Dynamic Bible):** Section 4 (pp. 59–75: *Texture Indication & Surface Reaction to Light*).
- 🌐 **Drawabox:** [Lesson 2: Texture Analysis & Dissections](https://drawabox.com/lesson/2/4).
- 📌 **Curated Pinterest Board:** [Texture & Rendering Board](https://pin.it/1E1KOFvOr).
- 📺 **Key Videos:**
  - Modern Day James: [Texture Drawing in Shape and Light](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y0XVPprbYY)
  - Tyler Edlin: [LEARN TO DRAW TEXTURE AND MATERIALS (Fundamental Friday)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-2xhmKLsPO8)
  - brokendraw: [Roadmap Video — Week 7 @ 29:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zYzgxUVSpUc&t=1774s)

#### Day-by-Day Schedule
- [ ] **Day 1: Texture as Cross-Contour Wrapping**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Cross-Contour Flow:** 5 organic bean volumes with cross-contour rings (Week 3 Ex 14).

  - Part B: Drill (50m) — **Purpose: Non-Flattening Patterns:** Draw 4 Organic Sausage forms. Wrap geometric patterns (stripes, checkerboards, stitch lines) around the surface contours without letting them flatten.
- [ ] **Day 2: The Texture Bar Exercise (Material Swatches)**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Gradient Control:** Drawing graduated hatching gradients.
  - Part B: Study (15m) — **Purpose: Tactile Translation:** Read brokendraw Ex 15 *Texture Bar* (p. 54) & Peter Han *Dynamic Bible* (p. 60–64).
  - Part B: Drill (60m) — **Purpose: Material Swatch Encoding:** Complete 5 Texture Bars:
    1. Smooth polished chrome / metal
    2. Chiseled cracked stone / rock
    3. Rough tree bark
    4. Reptile / fish scales
    5. Knitted woven fabric
- [ ] **Day 3: Textured Blobs (Organic Volume Dissections)**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Texture Speed:** 2 quick texture bars.

  - Part B: Drill (60m) — **Purpose: Volumetric Dissections:** Draw 5 Textured Blobs: Build an organic volume with wireframe lines, then wrap material textures around the surface (e.g. pineapple skin, dragon scales, braided rope).

  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Hatching Discipline:** Peter Han hatching warmup.
  - Part B: Drill (45m) — **Purpose: Material Fluency:** Draw 5 more Textured Blobs with varied textures (Total: 10 Textured Blobs hit! 🎉).
  - Part B: Study (30m) — **Purpose: Graphic Ink Economy:** Collect 5 drawings by a master ink stylist (e.g. Chris Samnee, Mike Mignola, Moebius). Study how they simplify shadows into graphic black shapes.
- [ ] **Day 5: Full Reference Study Deconstruction**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Surface Recall:** 1 quick textured blob.
  - Part B: Drill (60m) — **Purpose: Complex Material Deconstruction:** Complete 3 Reference Studies: Choose photo references (e.g. an animal, an armored boot, a motorcycle engine). Deconstruct into volumes, mannequinize, and apply surface texture indication.
  - Part C: Project Step 1 (20m) — **Purpose: Style Fusion Selection:** Select reference photo and chosen artist style for Project #7.
- [ ] **Day 6: Project #7 Execution — Stylized Master Reference Fusion**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Mark Economy:** Quick ink economy mark-making.
  - Part C: Project (60m) — **Purpose: Stylized Master Fusion:** Take your reference photo (e.g. a cheetah or athlete). Construct the volumetric understructure, then render the entire piece filtered strictly through the mark-making style of your chosen master (e.g. Chris Samnee block blacks or Moebius clean hatch lines).
  - Weekly Review (15m) — **Purpose: Log:** Log hours in Section 5.
- [ ] **Day 7: The 50% Rule (Active Play / Rest)**

### WEEK 8: Scene Building & Capstone Drawing from Imagination

> **Primary Objective:** Synthesize every skill from the past 7 weeks into a cohesive, narrative illustration drawn from imagination. Master cognitive load management through the 4-Phase Pipeline, and lock in the lifelong Daily Highlights (AMRAP) habit.
> 
> **Why This Week Matters (The Purpose):** This is the culmination of the entire journey. Week 8 manages cognitive load through the 4-Phase Scene Building Pipeline, proving to you that drawing complex multi-character scenes from pure imagination is not magical talent—it is a systematic engineering process of idea $\to$ research $\to$ wireframe $\to$ finish.

#### References & Study Material
- 📖 **brokendraw Book:** Level 5 Imagination (pp. 72–76) — *Ex 22 Magical Observation* (p. 73), *Ex 23 POV Drawing* (p. 74), *Ex 24 Daily Highlight* (p. 75), *Ex 25 Comic Book* (p. 76).
- 📖 **Peter Han (The Dynamic Bible):** Section 4 (pp. 70–79: *Final Scene Staging and Storytelling*).
- 📺 **Key Videos:**
  - Rembert Montald (Proko): [How to Invent Scenes From Imagination](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fSCz8akhJzw)
  - brokendraw: [5 levels of drawing from imagination (to improve faster)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_YuSTSfl-7s)
  - brokendraw: [How I draw Believable Scenes from Imagination: A 5 Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dWrT-MakDIc)
  - brokendraw: [Roadmap Video — Week 8 Scene Building Walkthrough @ 38:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zYzgxUVSpUc&t=2325s)

#### Day-by-Day Schedule
- [ ] **Day 1: Cognitive Stacking & POV Drawing**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Spatial Geometry:** 5 quick Y-method boxes from Week 2.
  - Part B: Study (20m) — **Purpose: Camera Elevation Theory:** Watch Rembert Montald *Thinking in 3D Space* & read brokendraw Ex 23 *POV Drawing* (p. 74).
  - Part B: Drill (50m) — **Purpose: Camera Angle Re-projection:** **Ex 23 Drill:** Draw an arrangement of 3 boxes on a table. Now draw the exact same arrangement from 3 different camera heights (Eye-level, Aerial bird's-eye, Ground frog's-eye).
- [ ] **Day 2: Scene Rotation & Master Copy**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Figure Projection:** 3 quick box figures in perspective.
  - Part B: Drill (45m) — **Purpose: Mental Scene Orbit:** **Scene Rotation Sheet:** Draw a simple scene cluster (a character sitting on a crate next to a lamp post). Re-draw the entire scene from an angle 90 degrees to the side.
  - Part B: Drill (35m) — **Purpose: Master Composition Deconstruction:** Master Copy Study: Pick a Kim Jung Gi or Moebius scene. Deconstruct their camera angle, horizon line, and primary mannequin placements.
- [ ] **Day 3: Daily Highlight Habit (AMRAP - As Many Reps As Possible)**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Memory Calibration:** brokendraw Ex 24 *Daily Highlight* (p. 75).
  - Part B: Drill (60m) — **Purpose: AMRAP Visual Journaling:** Visual Journaling: Sketch 2 scenes from your own day completely from imagination (e.g. walking into a coffee shop, sitting at your desk, stuck in traffic). Focus on space first, figures second.
- [ ] **Day 4: Capstone Project #8 — Phase 1 (Idea) & Phase 2 (Research)**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Perspective Warmup:** Quick perspective warmup.
  - Part C: Capstone Phase 1 (30m) — **Purpose: Narrative Premise Definition:** **The Idea Statement:** Formulate your narrative sentence (*"A tense moment on a creaky wooden harbor dock where family friends struggle to land an oversized thrashing fish."*).
  - Part C: Capstone Phase 2 (50m) — **Purpose: Targeted Visual Library Ingestion:** **Visual Library Research:** Gather 10–15 specific references for unfamiliar props, lighting, environment structures, and costumes. Sketch quick thumbnail breakdowns of the unfamiliar objects.
- [ ] **Day 5: Capstone Project #8 — Phase 3 (Thumbnails & Spatial Architecture)**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Thumbnail Framing:** 3 quick thumbnail boxes.
  - Part C: Capstone Phase 3 (70m) — **Purpose: Spatial Architecture & Camera Locking:** Draw 4 small composition thumbnails:
    1. Lock in horizon line and camera focal height.
    2. Establish perspective convergence.
    3. Block in all mannequins (head-ribcage-pelvis orientation).
    4. Check visual hierarchy, negative space, and readability. Select the strongest thumbnail.
- [ ] **Day 6: Capstone Project #8 — Phase 4 (Refinement, Textures & Inking)**
  - Part A: Warm-up (10m) — **Purpose: Inking Line Hierarchy:** Inking mark-making calibration.
  - Part C: Capstone Phase 4 (90–120m) — **Purpose: Final Imaginative Illustration:** Scale up the winning thumbnail to a full-page illustration:
    1. Overlay anatomy on top of mannequins.
    2. Add clothing folds and draping (Week 6).
    3. Apply surface textures and material indication (Week 7).
    4. Finalize line weight hierarchy (bold silhouettes, medium contours, fine textures).
  - Weekly & Course Review (20m) — **Purpose: Completion Reflection:** Fill out final row in Section 5. Celebrate your transformation! 🎉
- [ ] **Day 7: The 50% Rule & Forever Habit**
  - Part D: Forever Habit — **Purpose: Lifelong Creative Engine:** Lock in the Daily Highlight habit (**Exercise 24**). Keep drawing from imagination every single day!
